How RAG works Step 1 of 6

1. A customer asks a question

Question"Can I return a TV after 20 days?"

The question is only text. The model has not seen the returns policy yet.

2. The question becomes a vector

Question"Can I return a TV after 20 days?"

An embedding model turns the question into a list of numbers:

[0.21, -0.48, 0.77, 0.09, -0.30, 0.35, 0.58, -0.12, ...]

Text with similar meaning gets similar numbers, so the question can be compared with stored text.

3. The policy was already split into chunks

Each chunk was embedded when the index was built.

Chunk ACustomers may return most items within 30 days of delivery.
vector: [0.14, -0.51, 0.83, ...]
Chunk BElectronics have a 15-day window and must include all accessories.
vector: [0.12, -0.53, 0.88, ...]
Chunk CSale items are final and cannot be returned.
vector: [-0.40, 0.22, -0.11, ...]

4. Compare the question with each chunk

Cosine similarity: higher means closer in meaning. Illustrative scores.

Chunk A
0.61
Chunk B
0.82
Chunk C
0.18

5. The best match goes into the prompt

Top matchChunk B: Electronics have a 15-day window and must include all accessories.
PromptAnswer only from the sources below. Cite them as [n].
Sources: [1] Electronics have a 15-day window...
Question: Can I return a TV after 20 days?

6. The model answers from the source

AnswerNo. Electronics have a 15-day return window [1].

A TV returned on day 20 is outside the window. The citation lets a person check the source in one step.
